  
  [1X2 [33X[0;0YHow the groups are built[133X[101X
  
  
  [1X2.1 [33X[0;0YFree products with embeddings[133X[101X
  
  [33X[0;0YEvery  group  of  this package is a quotient [23XQ = (G_1 * \cdots * G_n)/N[123X of a
  free  product  of copies of the input group [23XG[123X, where [23XN[123X is the normal closure
  of  the defining relations. The construction starts from [10XFreeProduct[110X of [5XGAP[105X,
  which  accepts  groups in any representation, and keeps the embeddings [23XG \to
  G_i[123X  given  by  [10XEmbedding[110X.  The  images  of  the generators of [23XG[123X are carried
  through  the  quotient  and through the conversion of [23XQ[123X into a pc group, and
  the embeddings [23XG \to Q[123X are stored with the result.[133X
  
  [33X[0;0YThis  is  what  makes  the subgroups of [23XQ[123X cheap to compute. For instance, in
  [23X\chi(G)[123X  the subgroups [23XL(G)[123X, [23XD(G)[123X, [23XW(G)[123X and [23XR(G)[123X are defined in terms of the
  two  copies  [23XG[123X and [23XG^\psi[123X and of the isomorphism [23X\psi[123X between them; with the
  embeddings  at  hand they are direct computations inside a pc group. Without
  them,  one has to locate the copies of [23XG[123X inside the computed group, which is
  a search, or to carry every element of [23XG[123X through a presentation by hand.[133X
  
  [33X[0;0YFor example, the pc group [23X\chi(Q_8)[123X below has [23X7[123X generators in its pcgs, with
  no  visible  relation  to the generators of [23XQ_8[123X; the embeddings give the two
  copies  of  [23XQ_8[123X inside it at once, and [23XD = [G, G^\psi][123X and [23XL[123X are then single
  commands:[133X
  
  [4X[32X  Example  [32X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XG := SmallGroup( 8, 4 );;   # the quaternion group[127X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XC := SidkiChiGroup( G );;[127X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XLength( Pcgs( C ) );[127X[104X
    [4X[28X7[128X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XH := Image( Embedding( C, 1 ) );;  Hpsi := Image( Embedding( C, 2 ) );;[127X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XSize( CommutatorSubgroup( H, Hpsi ) );   # D(G)[127X[104X
    [4X[28X2[128X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XSize( Subgroup( C, List( G, g ->[127X[104X
    [4X[25X>[125X [27X     Image( Embedding( C, 1 ), g )^-1 * Image( Embedding( C, 2 ), g ) ) ) );   # L(G)[127X[104X
    [4X[28X16[128X[104X
  [4X[32X[104X
  
  [33X[0;0YIn our tests, for the group of order [23X32[123X [10XSmallGroup(32,49)[110X, whose [23X\chi(G)[123X has
  order  [23X2^{21}[123X, [23X\chi(G)[123X is computed in about [23X0.1[123X seconds and [23XL(G)[123X and [23XD(G)[123X in
  a  few  milliseconds; computing [23XL(G)[123X by carrying the elements of [23XG[123X through a
  presentation,  as  in  the  author's  earlier  code,  did  not finish in ten
  minutes.[133X
  
  
  [1X2.2 [33X[0;0YStrategy by class of groups[133X[101X
  
  [33X[0;0YThe  finitely  presented  group  [23XQ[123X  is  not  a good group to compute in. The
  package  replaces  it by a group in which one can compute (a pc group, a pcp
  group or a subgroup of a direct product), choosing the construction from the
  class of [23XG[123X. In every case the embeddings of the copies of [23XG[123X are kept.[133X
  
  [33X[0;0YFor [23X\chi(G)[123X ([2XSidkiChiGroup[102X ([14X3.1-1[114X)) the methods are tried in this order:[133X
  
  [30X    [33X[0;6Y[23XG[123X  finite or polycyclic, generated by two elements, with trivial Schur
        multiplier: then [23XR(G) = 1[123X [KM21, Lemma 9.3] and [23XW(G)/R(G) \cong M(G) =
        1[123X  [Roc82],  so [23X\chi(G)[123X is the subgroup [23XT(G)[123X of [23XG \times G \times G[123X of
        Sidki [Sid80]. This includes non-solvable groups, such as [23XPSL(2,8)[123X.[133X
  
  [30X    [33X[0;6Y[23XG[123X  finite  and  perfect:  [23X\chi(G) = T(\tilde G)[123X, where [23X\tilde G[123X is the
        universal covering group of [23XG[123X [Sid80, Theorem 4.4.1].[133X
  
  [30X    [33X[0;6Y[23XG[123X  an  infinite polycyclic group generated by two elements: [23X\chi(G)[123X is
        the  group  [23X\tau(G)  =  \tau^0(G)[123X  of  Dias and Rocco [DR20], which is
        [23X\chi(G)/R(G)[123X,   and   [23XR(G)   =   1[123X.   For   other  polycyclic  groups,
        [2XSidkiChiModRGroup[102X ([14X3.2-1[114X) gives [23X\chi(G)/R(G)[123X.[133X
  
  [30X    [33X[0;6Y[23XG[123X  a finite [23Xp[123X-group: [23X\chi(G)[123X is a finite [23Xp[123X-group (Sidki), so it is the
        largest [23Xp[123X-quotient of [23XQ[123X, computed by [10XEpimorphismPGroup[110X without a bound
        on the class.[133X
  
  [30X    [33X[0;6Y[23XG[123X  finite and solvable: [23X\chi(G)[123X is a finite solvable group whose order
        involves  only  the  primes dividing [23X|G|[123X (Sidki), so it is the largest
        such solvable quotient of [23XQ[123X, computed by [10XEpimorphismSolvableQuotient[110X.[133X
  
  [33X[0;0YFor other groups, [2XSidkiChiModWGroup[102X ([14X3.3-1[114X) gives [23X\chi(G)/W(G)[123X as a subgroup
  of [23XG \times G \times G[123X.[133X
  
  [33X[0;0YThe  user  may also choose the method, with a second argument: [2XSidkiChiGroup[102X
  ([14X3.1-3[114X)  and  [2XRoccoNuGroup[102X  ([14X5.1-3[114X).  The  method  [10X"fp"[110X returns the finitely
  presented  group  itself,  for  any  finite  [23XG[123X,  leaving to the user what to
  compute in it.[133X
  
  [33X[0;0YFor  [23X\nu(G)[123X  ([2XRoccoNuGroup[102X  ([14X5.1-1[114X)):  if  [23XG[123X  is  a  finite [23Xp[123X-group, Rocco's
  presentation [Roc94], with [23Xx[123X, [23Xy[123X in a pc generating sequence, and the largest
  [23Xp[123X-quotient;  if  [23XG[123X  is  finite solvable or polycyclic, the nonabelian tensor
  square  of  the  package  Polycyclic  [EN08].  For  [23X\tau^q(G)[123X ([2XRoccoTauGroup[102X
  ([14X6.1-1[114X)):  a  polycyclic presentation following Dias and Rocco [DR20], for [23XG[123X
  finite  solvable  or  polycyclic.  For  [23X\chi(n,H)[123X  ([2XSidkiChiNGroup[102X ([14X4.1-1[114X)):
  [23XT_n(\tilde H)[123X if [23XH[123X is finite and perfect, and the largest [23Xp[123X-quotient if [23XH[123X is
  a finite [23Xp[123X-group.[133X

    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XSetInfoLevel( InfoSOR, 0 );[127X[104X

  [1X2.3 [33X[0;0YTime limits[133X[101X
  
  [33X[0;0YGAP  has  no  time  limit  for a computation inside the running session. The
  function  [10XIO_CallWithTimeout[110X  of  the  [5XIO[105X  package  (a separate package, not
  needed  by  SOR,  distributed  with GAP and loaded with [10XLoadPackage( "io" )[110X)
  runs  a  function  in  a copy of the session (a forked process) and stops it
  after  the  given time, returning [10X[ true, [110X[3Xresult[103X[10X ][110X, or [10X[ false ][110X if the time
  ran  out.  The  result  is sent back from the copy, which works for numbers,
  lists  and  strings  but not for groups: ask the copy for the invariants you
  need, and compute the group itself in the session (or take [10XSidkiChiGroup( G,
  "fp" )[110X) when it is worth it.[133X
  
  [4X[32X  Example  [32X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XLoadPackage( "io" );;[127X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XIO_CallWithTimeout( rec( seconds := 60 ),[127X[104X
    [4X[25X>[125X [27X       G -> Size( SidkiChiGroup( G ) ), SmallGroup( 8, 4 ) );[127X[104X
    [4X[28X[ true, 128 ][128X[104X
    [4X[25Xgap>[125X [27XIO_CallWithTimeout( rec( seconds := 1 ),[127X[104X
    [4X[25X>[125X [27X       function() while true do od; end );   # never finishes[127X[104X
    [4X[28X[ false ][128X[104X
